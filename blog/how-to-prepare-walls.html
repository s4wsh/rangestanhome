How to Prepare Your Walls Before Painting

Prep work is where most repainting projects win or fail. Here is the sequence our crew follows before opening any paint.

1. Wash and inspect

Dust, cooking residue, and fingerprints keep paint from bonding. We wash every wall with mild detergent, rinse with clean water, and inspect with a bright light and moisture meter. Anything above 16 percent moisture gets addressed before sanding.

2. Sand in stages

We start with 120-grit paper to knock down rough spots, then move to 220-grit for a softer touch. Sanding blocks keep pressure even, and vacuums run after every pass so dust never drifts through the house.

3. Repair and feather

Cracks are opened slightly into a V-shape, filled with fiber-reinforced compound, and sanded flush. Larger dents receive two thin coats of plaster instead of one thick layer. The goal is a smooth transition that disappears under primer.

4. Prime and mask

Primer seals repairs, equalizes absorption, and prevents flashing. While primer dries, trims, floors, and fixtures are masked with fresh tape and protective film. Every opening, outlet, and piece of furniture is labeled so cleanup is instant.

5. Final checks

Only after the surface is perfectly smooth do we open paint buckets. A five-minute pause at this stage prevents hours of fixing brush marks later.
